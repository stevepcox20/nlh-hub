01

What This Is

A worked example, not a new framework.

Jade posted this to his socials as "Inside The Method" — his own real coaching call, face and voice altered to protect the client's identity, with text cards overlaid at each key moment explaining what he's doing and why. The belief being worked is "I'm not good enough / I'm not a good enough man."

Everything here is craft, not curriculum. The frameworks (dimensionalizing, watcher stance, prediction error, Rewrite/Rewire) are already documented in the Coaching Methods manual and video pages. This page exists to show them running together, live, in the order a real session actually uses them — which is not always the order a manual lists them in.

Source: Jade Teta / Next Level Human, YouTube, "Watch a Real Identity Coaching Session: Rewriting 'I'm Not Good Enough.'" Full transcript and on-screen card text preserved in Section 9 below.

02

The Session Arc

Rewrite, then a touch of Rewire, in under five minutes.

Where this session sits in the three-phase model
1
Rewrite
Exit intellect → dimensionalize → watcher stance → surface the contradiction
2
Rewire
Raise an opposing feeling: Leader. Protector. Teacher.
3
Retrain
Happens after the session — the client's own closing words become the homework

Retrain isn't shown on camera — it's the repetition the client does afterward. The session's whole job is to set up what gets retrained.

The ten moves, in order

  1. Close the eyes — exit the intellect
  2. State the old story out loud, at full strength
  3. Dimensionalize: age, color, texture
  4. Follow the age down to the actual wound
  5. Shift to the watcher stance — coach it, don't be it
  6. Ask a question it can't easily answer, then hold silence
  7. Let the client surface his own contradiction
  8. Rewire: raise an opposing feeling, one identity word at a time
  9. Close on the client's own words, spoken for himself
  10. No certainty claimed — name what was attempted, not a guarantee
03

Rewrite: Exit The Intellect

Moves 1-2. Get out of the thinking brain before the belief is even named.

From the session

Jade: "I'm just going to close your eyes for me."

Jade: "And I really want you to internalize that you're not good enough. You're not a good enough man."

Why the eyes close first, before anything else. The story doesn't live in the intellect — it lives in the body. Closing the eyes is the very first move of the session, before the belief is even stated out loud. It's not a relaxation cue, it's a location change: get the client out of head-space so the work that follows lands somewhere it can actually change something.

Why the old story is spoken at full strength, not softened

Jade says the harsh version directly: "you're not a good enough man," not a hedged paraphrase. This isn't cruelty, it's mechanism. A subconscious story only rewrites while its emotion is active — that's the reconsolidation window. A cool, intellectual discussion of the belief never opens that window. Heat does. So the coach's first job with the belief is to make it hot, not to talk around it.

04

Dimensionalizing The Wound

Moves 3-4. Turn a vague feeling into something workable, then follow it to source.

From the session

Jade: "Does it have an age?"

Client: "...six. Six years old."

Jade: "Years old, okay. Does it have a color?"

Client: "Red."

Jade: "Mhm. What's the texture of it?"

Client: "Like spongy. It's pain."

Jade: "Okay, that's good. Stay there. Pain. What kind of pain? What is the pain? Where is the pain?"

Client: "It's like a deep, like an internal wound."

The sequence

Age, color, texture — asked fast, one after another, no pause for analysis. Same tool as the anthropomorphising/dimensionalizing method already documented in the Coaching Methods video notes, shown here running at conversational speed rather than as a slow guided meditation. The physical-description questions aren't the goal — they're the on-ramp that turns "a vague bad feeling" into an object the client can actually work with.

The age is the root, not a data point

Jade doesn't log "six years old" and move on. He follows it straight down to the wound itself — what kind of pain, where it lives. The age that surfaces during dimensionalizing is treated as the actual source. What the client presents as an adult problem ("I'm not good enough") is thirty years of habit sitting on top of something that installed at six.

05

The Watcher Stance

Moves 5-7. Coach the wound instead of being it, then let silence do its work.

From the session

Jade: "And I want you to coach it out loud. Like you're coaching somebody else in the room. Disconnect from it. It's not you. Coach it. Tell it —"

Client: "It needs to lay down these walls, these guardrails. Just lay down the walls so I can —"

Jade: "Okay, but if it does that, how can it be sure it will be safe?"

[silence held — not rushed]

Client: "I can tell it that I'm ready to, you know, that I'm a grown-up, not a kid anymore, and I'm ready to lay this down."

Coach it, don't be it

One line does the whole identity move: "coach it out loud, like you're coaching somebody else, disconnect from it, it's not you." The client stops being fused with the six-year-old's fear and starts relating to it from an adult, capable vantage point — the same fused-vs-in-relationship-with shift the Dimensionalization of Consciousness material already teaches, shown here as a single spoken instruction instead of a scripted protocol.

Don't rescue the silence

After the hardest question ("how can it be sure it will be safe?"), Jade doesn't fill the gap. The pause itself is a signal — the client has left the intellect and gone deeper into the subconscious. Rushing in to prompt or rephrase would pull him back out. This happens twice in the session (also right after the eyes close): holding silence is a deliberate coaching move, not a gap that needs managing.

06

The Prediction Error

The belief doesn't rewrite because the coach argues with it. It rewrites because the client contradicts it himself.

The client's own answer — "I'm a grown-up now, I'm ready to lay this down" — is the prediction error in action. The brain expected "not good enough" and got the opposite, generated from the client's own mouth, not from Jade telling him he's wrong.

"The prediction error: the brain expects 'not good enough' and meets the opposite. No guarantee… but the hotter the emotion, and the more he surfaces his own contradiction, the more likely the rewrite."

Self-generated contradiction, not coach reassurance

This is the mechanism that separates real rewrite work from a pep talk. "You ARE good enough" from the coach is external reassurance — it doesn't touch the reconsolidation window. The client discovering and stating his own contradiction, while the emotion is still hot, is what actually has a shot at changing the story. Two conditions stack: heat, and self-generation. Either one alone is weaker than both together.

07

Rewire: Opposing Identity, One Word At A Time

Move 8. Not one sentence of opposing belief — three separate identity words, each confirmed and felt.

From the session

Jade: "Let me ask you this. Are you a leader?"

Client: "Uh…"

Jade: "You're a leader. You're a leader. Feel what that feels like."

Jade: "Are you a protector?"

Client: "Yes."

Jade: "Yes. You feel what that feels like."

Jade: "Are you a teacher?"

Client: "Yes."

Jade: "Yes. You're a teacher. Feel what that feels like."

Short words, stacked and separately confirmed

This is the Rewire step: he raises a feeling that opposes the old story. Not one long affirmation ("you're actually a good enough man") — three short, separate identity words (Leader. Protector. Teacher.), each one individually asked, individually confirmed, and individually felt in the body before moving to the next. A technique for Rewire not spelled out this specifically anywhere else in the manual: stack short, one-word identity confirmations rather than a single sentence of opposing belief.

08

Closing: His Words, Not Mine

Moves 9-10. The homework is whatever the client says for himself — and no certainty is claimed.

From the session

Jade: "Now, I want you to give yourself any final words that you need, and I want you to say it out loud, not for me, but for you, before you open your eyes."

Client: "Trust in this process." … "I am enough."

Client: "Yep, it was very powerful. I could feel it all through my body."

Jade: "If you're a coach, this is the level where change happens."

The homework-selection rule

"His words, not mine. Whatever he surfaces becomes the homework… the retrain he does after the session." The coach doesn't assign homework — the coach lets the client's own closing language become the homework. It has to be the client's own words, not the coach's, because self-generated language is what actually gets carried out and retrained through repetition afterward.

No certainty claimed. "A rewrite and a rewire. You can never be certain in the room… but that's what we're after." The method doesn't oversell a guaranteed result inside a single session — it names what was attempted and lets the real-world retrain confirm it or not.
