02

The Demo

Rania & Nicola. 6 weeks dead on social media, feast-and-famine pattern, real overwhelm underneath.

From the demo

Nicola: "Completely dead in the water for 6 weeks… there's no gas, there's no sail on the boat."

Rania: "What's burning you down right now? What's the big overwhelm in your life right now?"

Nicola: "I don't think we've had a day without a catastrophe this summer."

What Rania actually did, move by move (not a prescription — this is what happened)

  1. Let her run the full 4Ps herself, unprompted. She'd prepped and needed to just say it out loud.
  2. Skip the burnout story, ask what's underneath it. One question surfaces the real load: health scares, two houses, cars, non-stop crises.
  3. Check whether the goal relieves or adds to the load. "Does your business make you happy, or add to the overwhelm?" — Nicola separates the two: working with people = happy, social media = a means to an end, never happy.
  4. Land on the smallest possible action, not a strategy. "What's the smallest thing you can do that brings you this happiness without adding overwhelm?" She lands on it herself: a 3-line newsletter.
  5. Lock a date. "Tomorrow" beats "soon" every time.

"Sometimes it's as simple as: what is the smallest thing that we can do?"

What worked

Jade's live debrief: coaches often feel they have to deconstruct everything, get the client emotional, blow it all up and rebuild it inside one session. This demo proves the opposite. Naming the overwhelm and landing on the smallest possible next step was, in his words, "the perfect way to address it." Not every session needs root work — sometimes the correct intervention is scope reduction, not depth.

What could improve (Steve's read, not called out live)

Rania never checked for a charge. Nicola's own words carried real weight — "there is just nonstop catastrophes," health scares, a marriage under strain — and Rania's own reaction was "Wow." That's a moment where the guardrail step (stay here, feel this, what's coming up) belonged, even in a fast 10-minute session. Instead Rania stayed in problem-solving mode the whole way through: what's burning you down, does the business help or hurt, what's the smallest step. Useful, coherent coaching — but intellectual/logistics register from open to close, no somatic check at all.

The plan she landed (3-line newsletter, tomorrow) may well have been right. The miss is deciding that without ever testing whether there was more underneath — skipping the guardrail step isn't the same as confirming it doesn't apply.

03

Match The Register

Not every problem is a root-cause problem.

The tell

Nicola's own language was about volume and capacity ("dead in the water," "no gas," "burnt out"), not about a story or an identity wound. That's the signal to go logistics-first: name the overwhelm, find the smallest action, lock a date. Save the felt-sense/root-cause pathway for when the client's language is about a story, a pattern with a history, or an identity charge — not simply too much on the plate.

Caveat: "the language sounds logistical" isn't the same as "there's definitely no charge underneath." Section 02 above flags this exact demo as an example of skipping the check rather than running it and getting a clear no. Match the register, but check first.

Worked example of the other register: Coaching Lab 03 covers the same call's second demo, where the presenting issue was identity/pattern-level, not logistics — and shows what the felt-sense pathway actually looks like, including the exact moment it can be missed.
