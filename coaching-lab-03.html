Working The Thread

Rose & Jade (volunteering as client). Presenting issue: compulsive creating, resistance to "doing."

From the demo — full question sequence, in order

Rose: "Can you give me an example of creating versus doing? Something recent?"

Rose: "Where is the pressure to perform coming from? Does that land in your body, or in your history?"

Jade: "As soon as you said that… it hit me right here."

Rose: "What happens when you did get tied down? By your brothers, your parents, other obligations?"

Rose: "Why do you think you're doing that to yourself now? Do you feel like you don't deserve freedom? Or you haven't earned it?" (three questions, one breath)

Rose: "Does that bring up what you've been writing about, with your kidney situation?"

Jade: "It doesn't… it doesn't come through." (response to a long reassurance stretch — see critique below, not reproduced here)

Rose: "Is there any part of you that is afraid of the feeling of completion?"

What Rose actually did, move by move (not a prescription — this is what happened, including the misses)

  1. Ground the abstract complaint in one recent example ("creating vs. doing" → the 20 unpublished books).
  2. Ask directly where the pressure comes from — body or history — no hedging. Jade: "it hit me right here" — a felt charge, live, right there.
  3. Follow the thread to its origin — childhood resistance to his brothers and parents, a "freedom" pattern. Verbal, not into the body.
  4. Ask what happens when he does get tied down. He makes it miserable for everyone until they stop trying.
  5. Stack three questions in one breath instead of one — why pressure yourself / don't deserve freedom / haven't earned it. Jade answers the easiest one.
  6. Pull in outside knowledge ("your kidney situation") — only works because Rose knows Jade as a friend; not a repeatable move with a stranger.
  7. Switch from questions to a reassurance speech — Jade Teta comparison, book count, manifestation metaphor. Jade's own answer: "it doesn't come through."
  8. Recover with a sharp question: is he afraid of completion itself? Lands hard: "if I'm complete, it's time to go." Jade names the real unresolved thing: intellect hasn't met spirit yet.

Steve's alternate thread at step 3 (his own read, not tried live)

Right where Jade says "it hit me right here," that's the first guardrail moment in this whole demo — before "tied down" even comes up. Nobody named it live.

Steve's instinct: reflect "tied down" itself back — "what is that? Tell me more. What is it about feeling tied down?" — then test a hypothesis rather than assume it: "could it be you don't like being held to account for things, or am I off?" That's good Pattern-work: his own words, reflected, plus a tentative guess offered for him to confirm or correct (same shape as Rose's later "afraid of completion" question, which worked).

The catch: by the time "tied down" comes up, the charge already fired ("it hit me right here"). Strictly by the guardrail rule built into this system, the sharper move at that exact second is the body, not another verbal question — "you said it hit you right here, stay there, where, what's the temperature" — before following the story further. Steve's follow-up thread is smart Pattern-work, but it's still one beat behind the felt-sense opening.

03

The Miss, Named Live

Excellent questions. Wrong move at the exact second the charge showed up.

Jade's own debrief

Jade: "When I went feeling… that would have been a place where you'd be like, 'stay right there.' Close your eyes, feel that, tell me what's coming up. We may have gotten something much deeper subconsciously."

What worked

Rose's questions were sharp enough that Jade "couldn't intellectualize his way around them" — genuinely good coaching craft. She got real material fast: a concrete example, a body/history split, a childhood origin, and a felt response, all inside a few minutes.

What could improve

But the moment he actually dropped into a feeling (the sadness / "not enough time" beat), that was the cue to stop asking and switch to an instruction: close the eyes, stay with it, ask what's coming up in the body. Rose kept the conversation verbal instead — continuing to ask the next question rather than pausing there.

This is the same rule as Coaching Lab 01, live in a second demo. "Client gets emotional → coach asks a question about it" is the intellectual bypass Lab 01 named. This is what it looks like happening to a genuinely skilled coach mid-session — not a beginner mistake, an easy one.

A second entry point (Rania's own feedback)

When Jade named the block directly — "my intellect hasn't met my spirit" — that was a second place to go deeper: where does that specific block come from, which part of childhood. Rose moved on instead. Not a failure — many valid entry points exist in the same conversation, and not taking one is a road not travelled, not a wrong turn.

The miss nobody named live (Steve's own read)

The live debrief praised "asked the right questions, on point" and stopped there. Two things in the transcript don't hold up against that praise:

The reassurance monologue. Between the kidney question and "afraid of completion," Rose asks nothing for close to a minute — instead she compares Jade to Jade Teta, lists his output, offers a manifestation metaphor. It's persuading, not coaching. And it's checkable: Jade's actual response was "it doesn't… it doesn't come through." He said outright it didn't land, and the room moved past that without naming it as the miss it was.

Three questions stacked in one breath ("why pressure yourself / don't deserve freedom / haven't earned it") let Jade answer whichever's easiest instead of the one that actually cuts. One question, held, would have been sharper.

Both real skill (the completion question, the body/history split) and these misses sit in the same 15 minutes. The room's read wasn't wrong, just incomplete — it graded the questions and missed the stretch that wasn't questions at all.

04

The Move After The Feeling

The actual gap this session answers: what comes after "stay here, feel this, what's coming up?" — and where it's actually leading.

Five things, in order

  1. Don't ask another question. The instinct after a feeling word or a body sensation is to ask "why do you think that is?" That's the bypass. The correct next move is an instruction, not a question.
  2. Go where the body already pointed. If the feeling has a location, connect locations directly — "what's in the gut that needs to come out through the throat?" — rather than returning to the verbal thread.
  3. Stay longer than feels comfortable. The biggest miss, in this session and in Coaching Lab 01's debrief both, is leaving the somatic moment too early to get back to the "real" topic.
  4. Once it visibly shifts, ask for the message. "If that feeling had something to tell you, what would it be?" / "What's it trying to protect you from?" This is the step that turns sensation into insight — without it, the body work has no destination.
  5. Bridge out with the insight itself. "So what does that tell you about what you actually need right now?" That answer becomes the opening question for Possibilities. The body isn't the endpoint — it's the shortcut past whatever story he already has words for, and the message it hands you is the actual material.

Where this is going, in one line: body work exists to produce one true sentence he didn't have before. Stay in the body forever and it's therapy, not coaching. Skip the body and reason your way to the sentence, and you get the reassurance monologue above — an answer that sounds right and doesn't land, because he didn't find it, it was given to him.

Jade's own caveat, worth keeping: "You don't always have to… I don't want everyone to be like, every time I see a feeling, I've got to go in." Not every feeling needs the full sequence — this is what to do when you choose to go there, not a rule to run on every flicker.

Where this lives day to day: the full version of this sequence (including the message/bridge steps above) is in the Group Coaching Live Flow Guide Google Doc — that's the canonical, editable copy Steve actually uses live. The version on this page is the study record of where it came from.
