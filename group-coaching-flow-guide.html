Live Session Guide · Not The Cheat Sheet

Group Coaching — Live Flow

Follow this while you're actually running a session. For the full study reference (why it works, diagnostics, edge cases), use the Cheat Sheet. This is only: where to go next, what to say, and where you're most likely to slip into intellectual coaching.

The one rule underneath every step Find the charge. Don't talk about it. If you're asking "tell me more" or "why" — stop.

Can't find the Problem? Start here.

When someone's opener is vague or scattered, run it through the four life areas. Ask which one is loudest right now — that's your Problem step.

Identity "Who do you think you have to be right now — and where did that come from?"
Body "Is your body running on stress, or is it actually supported right now?"
Relationships "Where are you not being fully honest, or not fully seen, by someone close to you?"
Money / Work "Where are you avoiding, over-functioning, or under-charging?"

Don't run all four every time — this is a quick scan when you're stuck, not a checklist for every session. One clear "yes, that one" beats a client answering all four in the abstract.

  1. 1
    Open
    Get in fast. No catch-up.
    "If I could snap my fingers and make this disappear right now, what's going on for you?"

    Skip "how's it been going." Straight in.

    Live example — also fineNeither Coaching Lab demo used this exact line. Nicola came in already carrying it and just let it out unprompted — if a client walks in ready, let them go, don't force the script. Use the line above when they don't. (Lab 02)
  2. 2
    Problem
    Name it. Don't dig for detail.
    "What's the actual problem right now?"

    You need the shape of it, not the full story. Move on as soon as you have it.

    Live exampleNicola: "Completely dead in the water for 6 weeks… there's no gas, there's no sail on the boat." Volume/capacity language — the tell for a logistics-register problem, not an identity one. (Lab 02)
  3. 3
    Pattern
    Find the repeat. Where else does this show up?
    "When this happens, what do you find yourself doing? Where else does this show up in your life?"

    Reflect their own words back open-ended. Don't name the root for them — ask if it's familiar and let them confirm it.

    Live exampleRose: "Where is the pressure to perform coming from — does that land in your body, or in your history?" Direct, unhedged, and open — not "is this about your father?" Jade confirms the childhood link himself. (Lab 03)
  4. !
    Guardrail — the charge just showed up
    Stop. This is the moment coaches lose it.
    "Stay here. Feel this. What's coming up?"
    Do not ask "tell me more about that." That's the intellectual bypass. Get the feeling's location, temperature, texture. Let eyes close if it helps them go deeper instead of talking their way out.

    Then what? Once you've got location/temperature/texture — don't ask another question. Give an instruction instead: "stay with that," "let it move." If it has a body location, connect it directly ("what's in the gut that needs to come out through the throat?"). Stay longer than feels comfortable — the common miss is going back to the topic before the charge has actually shifted.

    Live example — missed itJade dropped into sadness ("so much work to do, not enough time"). Rose kept asking questions instead of switching to an instruction here. Jade's own words after: "that would have been a place where you'd be like, stay right there." (Lab 03)
    Live example — skipped it entirelyNicola named a summer of nonstop crises (health scares, houses, cars) — real weight, Rania's own reaction was "Wow." But Rania went straight to logistics (smallest next step) without ever running this step at all. Worked out fine here, but it's a guess, not a check. (Lab 02)
  5. 4
    Possibilities
    Wait for it. Don't hand it over.
    "If this pattern wasn't running the show — what's possible? What's this here to teach you?"
    Silence is the work. Don't fill it. If they surface the answer themselves, that's the real shift. Only give it to them if they truly can't find it — and know that's the smaller win, not the goal.
    Live exampleRania: "Does your business make you happy, or add to the overwhelm?" — Nicola separates the two herself: working with people = happy, social media = never happy, just a means to an end. The possibility (what actually matters here) came from her, not Rania. (Lab 02)
  6. 5
    Plan
    One thing. Checkable.
    "What's the one thing you'll actually do about this before we talk again?"

    One action, not a list. Specific enough you could ask them next time whether they did it.

    Live example"A 3-line newsletter." "When can you do that?" "Tomorrow." Small, specific, dated — not a strategy. (Lab 02)
  7. 6
    Close
    Recap in one line. Move on.
    "So: [problem] → [pattern] → what you're taking → [plan]."

    Keep it tight. Next volunteer.

When to stop using this page When Step 3 → Guardrail → Step 4 happens without you thinking about it. That's the whole guide, internalised.
